Smart persistence rescales yesterday's output by the ratio of clear-sky GHI between the two days. At a one-day lag that ratio is close to one, so the two persistence references differ by less than 0.5\% of RMSE. The per-rooftop scale factor is fitted by least squares on each rooftop's training split (median 0.904, range 0.513--1.662), so that variant uses local history and is not a zero-shot method. Across the 33 rooftops, the bias of the nameplate physical chain correlates with the capacity ratio $P_{99}/C_\mathrm{p}$ at +0.850, and its $R^2$ correlates with the zero-shot learner's $R^2$ at +0.971; the zero-shot learner has the higher $R^2$ on 25 of 33 rooftops. Wind speed from Open-Meteo is served in km\,h$^{-1}$ and is converted to m\,s$^{-1}$; plane-of-array irradiance is capped at 1400~W\,m$^{-2}$ to remove rare transposition spikes at low sun.

\section{Computation}
\label{sec:s-compute}

All experiments ran on one workstation with 12 logical CPU cores (Python 3.12, LightGBM 4.6.0, scikit-learn 1.9.0, pandas 3.0.3, pvlib). The three temporal searches took 145~min in total (42.8, 59.5 and 43.0~min) and the transfer search 21~min. Every result after the timing correction is regenerated by \path{run_review_pipeline.sh}. LightGBM's OpenCL GPU backend was 3.0 times slower than the CPU on this workload (47.4~s against 15.8~s for 300 trees on the training split). With 13 features there is too little histogram work per row for the GPU to recover its kernel-launch overhead.
