\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

For rooftops seen in training, day-ahead forecasting is limited mainly by the NWP input. Four analyses on 33 Belgian rooftops, over a February--May test window, are consistent with this:
\begin{enumerate}
\item knowledge of the local sky would add at least 0.065 in $R^2$, fleet-wide knowledge only 0.019;
\item residuals decorrelate within a day, consistent with cloud-field error;
\item irradiance error is equally large at every rooftop but correlated only over tens of kilometres;
\item removing 70\% of the irradiance bias leaves PV accuracy unchanged on seen and unseen rooftops.
\end{enumerate}

Within that limit, gains are small. Averaging forecasts across six centres helps; averaging one centre's re-runs does not, and ensemble dispersion hurts. Recalibration fixes P90 coverage (0.916) but worsens P25 and P50 and leaves pinball loss unchanged.

Two results apply beyond this dataset. Validating against a reanalysis from the same model understates RMSE 1.39-fold relative to an independent satellite record. Hyperparameters chosen where every unit is seen transfer poorly; selecting them against held-out systems halves the failures on unseen rooftops.
