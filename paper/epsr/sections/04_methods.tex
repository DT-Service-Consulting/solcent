\subsection{Forecast task and baseline}
\label{sec:meth-task}

For each system and each 15-min daylight slot of the following day, the model predicts output normalised by rated capacity,
\begin{equation}
y = P / (1000\,C_\mathrm{p}),
\end{equation}
with $P$ the AC output in W and $C_\mathrm{p}$ the rated DC capacity in kW$_\mathrm{p}$, clipped to $[0, 1.2]$ because real systems briefly exceed nameplate in cold, bright conditions. The forecast is issued around noon of the preceding day and uses only information available then. The reference forecast is persistence, the same slot one day earlier, and skill is
\begin{equation}
S = 1 - \mathrm{RMSE}_\mathrm{model} / \mathrm{RMSE}_\mathrm{persistence}.
\end{equation}
Skill is the headline metric (Section~\ref{sec:rw-prob}). Part of the previous day is not yet observed at noon, so this persistence is slightly optimistic and the reported skill conservative. Two further references need no training: smart persistence, rescaled by the ratio of clear-sky GHI between the two days, and a physical chain from forecast plane-of-array irradiance through the Faiman cell-temperature model to PVWatts with 14\% losses, driven by nameplate metadata alone.

\subsection{Features}
\label{sec:meth-features}

Thirteen features enter the final model (Table~\ref{tab:features}). The sine of time of day was dropped: it ranked lowest in permutation importance, and solar azimuth separates morning from afternoon. Plane-of-array irradiance uses the Hay--Davies transposition \citep{Hay1979} in pvlib \citep{Holmgren2018} with the reported tilt and azimuth. The only site-specific history is a lagged performance ratio,
\begin{equation}
pr = y \,/\, \max(\mathrm{GHI}_\mathrm{cs}/1000,\; 0.05),
\end{equation}
taken at the same slot two days earlier, because the previous day is incomplete at noon. It carries persistent site properties such as effective capacity, shading and soiling without letting yesterday's weather bias tomorrow's forecast.

\subsection{Model and hyperparameter selection}
\label{sec:meth-model}

Forecasts come from LightGBM gradient-boosted trees \citep{Ke2017,Friedman2001}. The learner was chosen in a preliminary benchmark on the same 33 systems and test period, run before the timing correction of Section~\ref{sec:data-nwp}, with 12 features, a squared-error objective and 20 tuning trials per learner: XGBoost, LightGBM and histogram gradient boosting reached test $R^2$ 0.801--0.804, random forest 0.794 and linear models at most 0.586. LightGBM was within 0.001 of the best and trained fastest (Supplementary material). One model is fitted for each quantile $\alpha \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.90\}$, each minimising the pinball loss
\begin{equation}
L_\alpha(y, \hat{y}) = \max\left[\alpha (y - \hat{y}),\; (\alpha - 1)(y - \hat{y})\right],
\end{equation}
whose minimiser is the conditional $\alpha$-quantile \citep{Koenker1978}. The quantiles follow the decisions the forecast serves rather than a symmetric interval: P25 is a conservative generation estimate for battery scheduling, where over-forecasting leaves a battery uncharged and forces peak-price imports; P50 is the point forecast; P90 bounds export. Their band has nominal coverage 0.65. A P10 model, with the P25 hyperparameters, completes an 80\% band for comparison with common practice. Quantiles are fitted independently and not reordered; they cross on 3.2\% of test rows before and 1.7\% after recalibration.

Two hyperparameter searches use a tree-structured Parzen estimator in Optuna \citep{Akiba2019}. The \textbf{temporal search} (known rooftops) runs sixty trials per quantile minimising that quantile's pinball loss on the validation split, with early stopping on the last 15\% of the training split; it selects depths of 11, 15 and 20 for P25, P50 and P90 and up to 2000 trees. E0 compares it with a preliminary configuration from 20 trials minimising validation RMSE, shared by all quantiles. The \textbf{transfer search} (unseen rooftops) holds out whole systems instead of future dates: the systems of a 45\% row subsample of the training and validation periods form three folds of eleven, and each of forty trials trains on two folds and is scored by P50 pinball loss on the third. It selects depth 4, L2 penalty 4.34 and 1500 trees. The temporal search excluded system 81376, flagged from its test-split error (Section~\ref{sec:res-config}). The temporal configuration is used on the chronological split, the transfer configuration for P50 in every leave-one-system-out experiment.

\subsection{Ensemble constructions}
\label{sec:meth-ens}

Three ways of widening the weather input are compared, all at a day-ahead lead time. Seven ECMWF runs issued one to seven days before the target and the day-ahead forecasts of five other centres form twelve members. A \emph{lagged mean} averages the ECMWF runs issued one to three days before the target. A \emph{multi-model mean} averages the day-ahead GHI of the six centres, ignoring missing members (feature 6). \emph{Ensemble dispersion} supplies standard deviations across all, lagged and multi-centre members, the interquartile range and the share of members within $\pm 15\%$ of the mean.

\subsection{Weather oracles}
\label{sec:meth-oracle}

To bound the value of better weather knowledge, the fleet oracle for system $i$ at time $t$ is the mean measured output of all other systems at the same instant,
\begin{equation}
o(i, t) = \frac{\sum_j y(j, t) - y(i, t)}{n(t) - 1},
\end{equation}
with $n(t)$ the number of systems reporting, used only where $n(t) > 3$. It describes fleet-wide rather than local cloudiness. Two local variants average instead over the other rooftops of the same region, or over the three nearest rooftops, and a capacity-normalised variant first divides each rooftop by its training-period $P_{99}$. An oracle is a proxy for the sky above a rooftop, so its gain is a lower bound on the value of that knowledge. Arms are compared on identical rows: the baseline, the baseline plus an oracle, and an oracle with solar geometry and metadata but no NWP.

\subsection{Verification and recalibration}
\label{sec:meth-verif}

Point accuracy is reported as MAE, RMSE, mean bias error (MBE, observed minus predicted), $R^2$ and skill; irradiance bias is forecast minus reference, following \citet{Tuononen2019}. Probabilistic quality is reported as the mean pinball loss over the three quantiles, a proper score \citep{Gneiting2007}, as reliability \citep{Lauret2019}, and as coverage of the P25--P90 band against its nominal 0.65. Paired differences carry 95\% intervals from a day-block bootstrap (2000 resamples of the 107 test days) and Diebold--Mariano tests on daily loss differentials with Newey--West variance \citep{Diebold1995}; leave-one-system-out comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across rooftops. Errors are stratified by the \emph{forecast} clear-sky index, which is known when decisions are made.

A per-quantile offset, in the spirit of \citet{Romano2019} but without its coverage guarantee, is fitted on the validation split and applied unchanged to the test split,
\begin{equation}
\delta_q = \mathrm{Quantile}_q\!\left(y_\mathrm{val} - \hat{y}_{\mathrm{val},q}\right), \qquad \hat{y}_{\mathrm{test},q} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\hat{y}_{\mathrm{test},q} + \delta_q,\; 0,\; 1.2\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Model output statistics}
\label{sec:meth-mos}

MOS is tested in two stages. At the \emph{irradiance stage}, a model learns the error of the hourly day-ahead ECMWF GHI, truth minus forecast, from ten predictors available at issue time: ECMWF GHI, cloud cover and temperature, clear-sky GHI, the forecast clear-sky index, solar elevation, and sine--cosine pairs of time of day and day of year. Ridge regression, random forest, LightGBM and XGBoost are compared, with SARAH-3 or ERA5 as truth. At the \emph{power stage}, the corrected irradiance enters the PV model. It is produced out of fold (training rows corrected by models fitted on the other four of five chronological folds, test rows by a model fitted on the full training split) and applied to the 15-min GHI and plane-of-array irradiance as a ratio.

\subsection{Leave-one-system-out and local calibration}
\label{sec:meth-loso}

Each system is held out in turn. A model trained on the other 32 systems over the training and validation periods predicts the held-out system's test window, giving 33 zero-shot results. The first $N \in \{3, 7, 14, 30, 45, 60\}$ days of that window then fit a local correction, and the rest is scored. Four corrections are compared: none; a \emph{bias} offset equal to the mean residual; \emph{linear} rescaling, $y \approx a + b\hat{y}$; and \emph{capacity + bias}, which first rescales by the observed capacity relative to the fleet,
\begin{equation}
s = \frac{P_{99,\mathrm{cal}} / C_\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{median}_\mathrm{fleet}\left(P_{99} / C_\mathrm{p}\right)}, \qquad s \in [0.4, 2.5],
\end{equation}
with the fleet median taken from training systems only, and then removes the residual offset. The evaluation window shrinks and moves later in the season as $N$ grows, so corrections are compared only at equal $N$.

\subsection{Experiments}
\label{sec:meth-exp}

Experiments E0--E5 and MOS each vary one factor and hold the rest fixed. Robustness checks train on data before each of four 90-day windows, start local calibration on four dates and score a fixed later window, and remove site features. The Supplementary material lists the experiments and gives hyperparameters, full result tables and computation details.
