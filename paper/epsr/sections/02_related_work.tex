\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learners, fleets and spatial aggregation}
\label{sec:rw-learners}

On distributed PV, a larger and more widely spread fleet improved day-ahead accuracy more than the choice among twelve models \citep{Visser2022}. Aggregation cancels part of the error of separated sites \citep{Aldeman2023}; an individual rooftop gets no such cancellation. Tree ensembles were the best learners on 152 distributed systems \citep{Visser2019}, and on tabular data of this size gradient-boosted trees remain state-of-the-art against deep architectures \citep{Grinsztajn2022}.

A fleet is also a sensor network. \citet{Lonij2013} used 80 residential rooftops as irradiance sensors, \citet{Elsinga2017} exploited time-lagged correlation between systems shaded by the same cloud, and \citet{Bessa2015} fed distributed generation into a vector autoregression. The gain is concentrated at intra-hour to few-hour horizons. Neighbour output is unavailable a day ahead; the oracles of Section~\ref{sec:meth-oracle} supply it on purpose, to bound its worth.

\subsection{Structure of NWP irradiance error}
\label{sec:rw-nwp}

How much NWP error post-processing can remove depends on how much is systematic. Against four years of co-located ceilometer and pyranometer data at Helsinki, ECMWF IFS day-ahead GHI had an overall bias of +8~W\,m$^{-2}$, which grew from +16 to +70~W\,m$^{-2}$ as cloud liquid water path fell \citep{Tuononen2019}: cloud is placed adequately but made too transparent, a systematic and learnable error. Two further error types are not learnable from the forecast alone. \emph{Displacement} error puts cloud in the wrong place or at the wrong time; under broken cloud, surface irradiance depends on where a shadow falls, at scales of kilometres and minutes \citep{Mol2024}, which a 25~km hourly field cannot resolve. \emph{Amplitude} error makes cloud too thick or too thin on a given day, beyond its systematic average. Irradiance and power are usually evaluated in separate studies, although the NWP source affects PV accuracy materially \citep{Mayer2023}, so whether an irradiance gain survives conversion to power is rarely tested.

\subsection{Validation reference}
\label{sec:rw-reference}

ERA5 is not a neutral reference. Against 41 BSRN stations, 294 European stations and two satellite products, it had a positive GHI bias of +4.54~W\,m$^{-2}$ over Europe, overestimating under cloud, with larger absolute errors than the satellite products \citep{Urraca2018}. It also shares the model of the forecast it is used to score.

SARAH-3 retrieves surface solar radiation from Meteosat imagery at 0.05$^\circ$ and 30-min resolution \citep{Pfeifroth2024,Pfeifroth2023}. Against 313 European pyranometers, CM SAF products show mean absolute deviation of daily means of 8--13~W\,m$^{-2}$ in central Europe \citep{Urraca2017}, although these figures do not cover sub-daily resolution. SARAH-3 also resolves variability that a 0.25$^\circ$ forecast cannot represent, so part of the error measured against it reflects resolution. To our knowledge, the effect of the reference choice on day-ahead error has not been quantified on the same data.

\subsection{Probabilistic forecasting and what to report}
\label{sec:rw-prob}

For solar irradiance, \citet{Lauret2019} separate calibration from sharpness and recommend proper scores \citep{Gneiting2007} such as CRPS and the pinball loss. For deterministic day-ahead forecasts, only skill against a stated, standard reference is comparable across studies \citep{Yang2019,Yang2020c}. $R^2$ alone is a poor headline for rooftop output, because most of its variance is the diurnal and seasonal cycle that astronomy supplies for free.

Ensemble NWP underpins most probabilistic solar forecasting \citep{Wang2022}, with routine calibration of under-dispersed ensembles \citep{YangYangLiu2023}; whether dispersion helps a quantile regressor already conditioned on clear-sky index is untested.

\subsection{Transfer to unseen sites and metadata quality}
\label{sec:rw-transfer}

SolNet \citep{Depoortere2025} is the closest comparator. It pretrains an LSTM on synthetic PVGIS output for each target location and fine-tunes it on local data. On 40 Dutch and 300 Australian homes and two Belgian commercial sites, the transferred model beat a locally trained one until about half a year of local data had accumulated, and mis-specifying coordinates by 200~km removed its advantage within two months. We compare with its reported results (Section~\ref{sec:disc-transfer} and Supplementary material) rather than rerun it, because its task differs: SolNet forecasts 24 hourly values from midnight, conditioned on the preceding 24~h of measured output and on the 00:00 ICON run, which its authors note is published only hours later; our forecast is issued at noon for the following day at 15-min resolution, before any of that day's output is known.

\citet{Killinger2018} found PVOutput.org, the source used here, strongly affected by implausible tilt entries, and volunteer records can also carry clock errors, gaps and export-only readings. Tilt and azimuth can be inferred from generation data \citep{Meng2020}. Rated capacity, which normalises the target of every fleet model, has received less attention, and its effect on transfer to unseen sites has not been characterised.
