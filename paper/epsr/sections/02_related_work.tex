\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learners, fleets and spatial aggregation}
\label{sec:rw-learners}

On distributed PV, a larger and more widely spread fleet improved day-ahead accuracy more than the choice among twelve models \citep{Visser2022}. Errors at separated sites are only partly correlated, so aggregation cancels part of them \citep{Aldeman2023}; an individual rooftop gets no such cancellation, so this study reports per-rooftop accuracy. Tree ensembles were the best learners on 152 distributed systems \citep{Visser2019}, and on tabular data of this size gradient-boosted trees remain state-of-the-art against deep architectures \citep{Grinsztajn2022}.

A fleet is also a sensor network. \citet{Lonij2013} used 80 residential rooftops as irradiance sensors, \citet{Elsinga2017} exploited time-lagged correlation between systems shaded by the same cloud, and \citet{Bessa2015} fed distributed generation into a vector autoregression. The gain is concentrated at intra-hour to few-hour horizons. Neighbour output is unavailable a day ahead; the oracles of Section~\ref{sec:meth-oracle} supply it on purpose, to bound its worth.

\subsection{Structure of NWP irradiance error}
\label{sec:rw-nwp}

How much NWP error post-processing can remove depends on how much is systematic. Against four years of co-located ceilometer and pyranometer data at Helsinki, ECMWF IFS day-ahead GHI had an overall bias of +8~W\,m$^{-2}$, which grew from +16 to +70~W\,m$^{-2}$ as cloud liquid water path fell \citep{Tuononen2019}: cloud is placed adequately but made too transparent, a systematic and learnable error. Two further error types are not learnable from the forecast alone. \emph{Displacement} error puts cloud in the wrong place or at the wrong time; under broken cloud, surface irradiance depends on where a shadow falls, at scales of kilometres and minutes \citep{Mol2024}, which a 25~km hourly field cannot resolve. \emph{Amplitude} error makes cloud too thick or too thin on a given day, beyond its systematic average. Irradiance and power are usually evaluated in separate studies, although the NWP source affects PV accuracy materially \citep{Mayer2023}, so whether an irradiance gain survives conversion to power is rarely tested.

\subsection{Validation reference}
\label{sec:rw-reference}

ERA5 is not a neutral reference. Against 41 BSRN stations, 294 European stations and two satellite products, it had a positive GHI bias of +4.54~W\,m$^{-2}$ over Europe, overestimating under cloud, with larger absolute errors than the satellite products \citep{Urraca2018}. It also shares the model of the forecast it is used to score.

SARAH-3 retrieves surface solar radiation from Meteosat imagery at 0.05$^\circ$ and 30-min resolution \citep{Pfeifroth2024,Pfeifroth2023}. Against 313 European pyranometers, CM SAF products show mean absolute deviation of daily means of 8--13~W\,m$^{-2}$ in central Europe \citep{Urraca2017}, although these figures do not cover sub-daily resolution. SARAH-3 also resolves variability that a 0.25$^\circ$ forecast cannot represent, so part of the error measured against it reflects resolution. What matters is errors independent of the forecast under test. To our knowledge, the effect of the reference choice on day-ahead error has not been quantified on the same data.

\subsection{Probabilistic forecasting and what to report}
\label{sec:rw-prob}

Proper scoring rules underpin probabilistic verification \citep{Gneiting2007}; for solar irradiance, \citet{Lauret2019} separate calibration from sharpness and recommend CRPS and the pinball loss. For deterministic day-ahead forecasts, only skill against a stated, standard reference is comparable across studies \citep{Yang2019,Yang2020c}. $R^2$ alone is a poor headline for rooftop output, because most of its variance is the diurnal and seasonal cycle that astronomy supplies for free.

Ensemble NWP underpins most operational probabilistic solar forecasting \citep{Wang2022}; raw ensembles are under-dispersed, so calibration is routine \citep{YangYangLiu2023}. \citet{MayerYang2023} paired the weather ensemble with physical model chains on 14 utility-scale plants. Such work assumes that dispersion carries information the point model lacks, which is untested for a quantile regressor conditioned on clear-sky index and solar geometry.

\subsection{Transfer to unseen sites and metadata quality}
\label{sec:rw-transfer}

\citet{Depoortere2025} is the closest comparator. On residential fleets in the Netherlands and Australia and commercial sites in Belgium, a transferred model beat a locally trained one until about half a year of local data had accumulated, and mis-specifying coordinates by 200~km removed the transfer advantage. Their SolNet pretrains on simulated PVGIS output for each target location and then fine-tunes on local data; it is not rerun here.

\citet{Killinger2018} found PVOutput.org, the source used here, strongly affected by implausible tilt entries, and volunteer records can also carry clock errors, gaps and export-only readings. Tilt and azimuth can be inferred from generation data \citep{Meng2020}, and physical model chains are sensitive to design-data quality \citep{Mayer2021}. Rated capacity, which normalises the target of every fleet model, has received less attention, and its effect on transfer to unseen sites has not been characterised.
