\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Rooftop fleet}
\label{sec:data-fleet}

The fleet comprises 33 residential PV systems in Belgium whose owners publish inverter data on PVOutput.org (Fig.~\ref{fig:fleet}). It spans about 180~km north--south and 210~km east--west in five regions: the West Flanders coast (7 systems), Antwerp (8), Brussels and the centre (8), Li\`ege (6) and the Ardennes (4). Rated capacity ranges from 2.04 to 10.53~kW$_\mathrm{p}$ (median 4.69, total 169.7~kW$_\mathrm{p}$). Generation, logged every 5~min for most systems and every 10 or 15~min for eight, is averaged to 15-min means. Local timestamps are localised to Europe/Brussels and joined to UTC weather data; the ambiguous autumn hour is dropped. Gaps of up to 30~min are interpolated, longer gaps are marked invalid, and no spike filter is applied. The study window runs from 1 April 2024 to 31 May 2026 and gives 1,158,011 valid daylight records. A slot counts as daylight when modelled clear-sky GHI exceeds 5~W\,m$^{-2}$, so night-time zeros do not inflate the metrics. One further system (12293) was excluded for its low share of valid records (64\%, against at least 80\% for every other). System 81376 is retained here and treated in Section~\ref{sec:res-config}.

\subsection{Installation metadata}
\label{sec:data-meta}

Owner-reported tilt, azimuth and rated capacity enter the model (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), and none survives inspection intact (Fig.~\ref{fig:metadata}). Nine systems report a tilt of 1$^\circ$ or less, three of them exactly 0$^\circ$. Nine flat roofs among 33 Belgian houses is implausible, and the round values indicate unfilled fields; our pipeline used them as reported. Azimuth is recorded only as a compass sector and takes three values: south (17 systems), south-west (9) and south-east (7). No east- or west-facing roof is present, and 180$^\circ$ stands for both a true southern aspect and an unreported one.

Rated capacity has a subtler problem. The ratio of each system's 99th-percentile output over the test period, $P_{99}$, to its nameplate capacity $C_\mathrm{p}$ has median 0.794 and range 0.568--1.273. Inverter clipping does not explain it: on the 31 systems with a plausible inverter rating, the capacity ratio correlates with the inverse DC/AC ratio at only +0.17. A low ratio can also come from shading, orientation or soiling, so it measures \emph{effective} capacity relative to nameplate rather than a capacity error alone. The highest ratio, 1.273, belongs to system 16111, which supplies every record above the target clip of 1.2 (0.13\% of all records).

\subsection{Weather forecasts}
\label{sec:data-nwp}

Day-ahead forecasts come from the Open-Meteo archive of operational NWP, retrieved with the \texttt{previous\_day1} suffix so that each value is the forecast issued one day before the target. No analysis field enters the features. The primary model is ECMWF IFS at 0.25$^\circ$. DWD ICON, NOAA GFS, M\'et\'eo-France, DMI and the UK Met Office supply the same variables at the same lead time, with full coverage except the Met Office (83.4\%). Seven successive ECMWF runs (\texttt{previous\_day1} to \texttt{previous\_day7}) form a lagged ensemble.

Hourly irradiance in the archive, as in ERA5 and SARAH-3, is a mean over the preceding hour. It is mapped to the 15-min grid through the clear-sky index: each hourly mean is divided by the modelled clear-sky mean over the same hour (with a 1~W\,m$^{-2}$ guard), the index is placed at the centre of the hour, interpolated linearly to the centre of each 15-min slot and multiplied by the clear-sky value there. This keeps the shape of the solar curve, which direct interpolation would flatten, and aligns forecast and target in time. The clear-sky model affects downstream results \citep{Yang2020a}; we use Ineichen--Perez \citep{Ineichen2002} through pvlib \citep{Holmgren2018}.

\subsection{Reference irradiance}
\label{sec:data-ref}

Irradiance forecasts are scored against two references. ERA5 \citep{Hersbach2020} follows common practice in distributed-PV studies. SARAH-3 \citep{Pfeifroth2024,Pfeifroth2023} is the independent reference (Section~\ref{sec:rw-reference}), with 99.2\% coverage over the study window; as a resolution check it is also averaged over the 0.25$^\circ$ forecast cell. Both are taken at each rooftop's coordinates, and irradiance is scored hourly against the day-ahead ECMWF forecast. No ground pyranometer is available for this fleet.

\subsection{Data splits}
\label{sec:data-splits}

The data are split chronologically: training from 1 April 2024 to 11 September 2025 (810,614 records), validation to 14 February 2026 (173,702) and test to 31 May 2026 (173,695). Validation is used only for hyperparameter selection and recalibration. Every system appears in all three partitions, so this split measures a fleet already under management. Deployment is measured separately by leave-one-system-out evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:meth-loso}).
