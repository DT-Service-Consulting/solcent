\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Where the limit lies}
\label{sec:res-limit}

\textbf{Selection objective (E0).} Selecting hyperparameters under the pinball loss instead of RMSE lowers mean pinball loss from 0.02566 to 0.02551 and raises skill from 0.4225 to 0.4251 (+0.0027, 95\% CI +0.0015 to +0.0038; Diebold--Mariano $p < 0.001$), about 40\% of what the ensemble feature adds (Section~\ref{sec:res-widening}).

\textbf{Weather oracles (E1).} Adding the contemporaneous output of every other rooftop raises $R^2$ from 0.8039 to 0.8233 (95\% CI of the gain +0.0121 to +0.0279) and skill from 0.4254 to 0.4546 (Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling}a). Without NWP the fleet oracle reaches only $R^2$ 0.7076. Local knowledge is worth far more: the mean output of the same region's rooftops, or of the three nearest ones (median distance 6.2~km), raises $R^2$ to 0.869 and skill to 0.530 on the same rows (Supplementary material). A capacity-normalised fleet oracle gives the same 0.8230, so the gap is not a capacity artefact. As the oracles are imperfect proxies for the sky above a rooftop, +0.065 is a lower bound on the value of local weather knowledge.

\textbf{Residual structure (E2).} The autocorrelation of P50 residuals, pooled over rooftops and paired at true time lags, is 0.711 at 15~min, 0.550 at 30~min, 0.404 at 1~h and 0.247 at 2~h, and falls to 0.108 at 24~h and 0.027 at 48~h (Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling}b). An error smooth over hours and nearly uncorrelated from day to day is neither bias nor noise; it is consistent with cloud that is displaced, or too thick or too thin, on a given day (Section~\ref{sec:rw-nwp}), and yesterday's error says almost nothing about today's.

\subsection{Where the error lives}
\label{sec:res-location}

Accuracy depends strongly on cloud (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}a). Binned by forecast clear-sky index, $R^2$ is 0.854 for clear slots (63\% of the test set) but 0.457--0.484 in the three broken-cloud bands, and 0.501 for overcast slots, whose target standard deviation is only 0.060.

Against SARAH-3, day-ahead irradiance error is largest in absolute terms under partly cloudy skies (RMSE 114.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, against 68.7 overcast), but relative to available light the order reverses: nMAE is 40.7\% overcast, 36.6\% partly cloudy and 15.9\% clear (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}b).

The size of the irradiance error is the same everywhere, but the errors themselves are local (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}c). Across the 33 rooftops the ratio of the standard deviation to the median of irradiance RMSE is 0.020 in partly cloudy conditions, 0.037 in clear and 0.064 in overcast. Yet the hourly irradiance errors of two rooftops correlate at a median of 0.92 within 10~km, 0.55 at 25--50~km and 0.19 at 150--250~km (Spearman $\rho = -0.98$ against distance over 528 pairs); PV residuals follow at 0.39, 0.23 and 0.05. The error is a property of the weather input at cloud scale, not of the site, which is why local oracles help and fleet averages little.

\subsection{Widening the weather input}
\label{sec:res-widening}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} gives the ensemble ablation (E3). Only the six-centre multi-model mean improves $R^2$, skill and pinball loss with intervals that exclude zero: skill rises from 0.4184 to 0.4251 (+0.0067, 95\% CI +0.0014 to +0.0119). The gain is small, and the arm was chosen from seven on the test split. Without the Met Office, the member with incomplete coverage, the mean of five centres keeps the pinball gain ($p = 0.006$) but not a significant skill gain. Averaging ECMWF's own runs from one to three days before the target is indistinguishable from the baseline ($p = 0.72$): re-runs of one model are not independent, whereas centres differ in physics and therefore in error. Ensemble dispersion as a predictor raises pinball loss by 1.2\% ($p = 0.002$) and lowers P25--P90 coverage from 0.618 to 0.588. In rolling 90-day windows trained only on earlier data, the multi-model mean helps in three of four seasons, by +0.008 to +0.012 skill, and costs 0.009 in May--July 2025 (Supplementary material).

\subsection{Proposed configuration and calibration}
\label{sec:res-config}

The proposed configuration (E4) is the base features plus the multi-model mean on all 33 systems: $R^2$ 0.8038, skill 0.4251, pinball loss 0.02551. One rooftop, 81376, holds 3.08\% of test rows but 7.87\% of squared error. In April--May 2026 its output relative to the fleet median fell to 0.60, against 0.85 and 0.89 in the same months of 2024 and 2025. The defect was found after the test errors were seen, so excluding it is only a sensitivity analysis. With 32 systems, $R^2$ rises by 0.0115 to 0.8152, about 2.5 times the gain of the best weather-input change.
%% Validation-period ratio of 81376 (system_drift_monthly.csv): Sep-Feb 2025/26 0.73, 0.84, 1.00, 1.08, 1.20, 0.98 vs 0.73, 0.69, 0.82, 0.80, 0.76, 0.78 a year earlier.

Raw quantiles under-cover at the upper tail: empirical coverage of the nominal P90 is 0.869 (Fig.~\ref{fig:recal}). Offsets fitted on validation ($+0.0006$, $+0.0026$ and $+0.0119$) move coverage of P25, P50 and P90 from 0.262, 0.494 and 0.869 to 0.272, 0.531 and 0.916: recalibration fixes P90 but worsens P25 and P50. Mean pinball loss is unchanged ($p = 0.82$), and P25--P90 coverage moves from 0.610 to 0.644 against the nominal 0.65. The P10 model is the least reliable quantile: 12.5\% of observations fall below it against a nominal 10\%, and its validation offset is negligible. The P10--P90 band covers 0.748 of observations before and 0.795 after recalibration against a nominal 0.80, with mean width 0.203 and 0.215 of capacity.

\subsection{Irradiance correction and the reference}
\label{sec:res-mos}

\textbf{Reference.} Over the study window, the same day-ahead ECMWF forecast scored against ERA5 has RMSE 69.2~W\,m$^{-2}$ and bias +17.8~W\,m$^{-2}$; against SARAH-3 it has 96.2~W\,m$^{-2}$ and +16.8~W\,m$^{-2}$. ERA5 understates RMSE by a factor of 1.39 (relative RMSE 23.6\% against 32.7\%) but not the bias. Averaged over the forecast's 0.25$^\circ$ cell, SARAH-3 gives 89.5~W\,m$^{-2}$, so resolution explains a quarter of the gap and leaves a factor of 1.29. SARAH-3 also tracks the rooftops better: hourly, it correlates with measured PV output at 0.846, against 0.808 for ERA5.
%% Sources: src/eval/reference_comparison.py (288,292 hourly rows, 33 systems); src/eval/reference_pv_corr.py.

\textbf{Irradiance stage.} On the test split, random forest lowers MAE against SARAH-3 from 58.6 to 56.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ ($-3.6\%$), whereas against ERA5 every learner raises MAE, by 5.8--14.8\%. Out of fold, the correction used in the PV model lowers MAE by 4.9\% and moves the bias from +12.2 to $-3.7$~W\,m$^{-2}$, removing 70\% of its magnitude.

\textbf{Power stage.} On seen rooftops, the corrected irradiance changes $R^2$ from 0.8038 to 0.8033 (95\% CI of the change $-0.0015$ to $+0.0007$) and raises pinball loss from 0.02551 to 0.02557 ($p = 0.02$); adding it as extra features is no better. On rooftops absent from training, where the model cannot have absorbed the bias, MOS should matter more. It does not: across 33 held-out rooftops the median change is +0.00015 in $R^2$, 18 of 33 improve (Wilcoxon $p = 0.65$), and changes range from $-0.043$ to $+0.017$. The reason is proportion. MOS removes most of a 12~W\,m$^{-2}$ systematic bias, but RMSE falls only from 90.2 to 86.5~W\,m$^{-2}$; the rest is the cloud-scale error of Section~\ref{sec:res-location}, and the PV model has already fitted around the systematic part.

\subsection{Deployment on an unseen rooftop}
\label{sec:res-deploy}

A rooftop absent from training receives median $R^2$ 0.741 and skill 0.338, against 0.804 and 0.425 pooled over known rooftops. Mean $R^2$ is 0.660, the 10th percentile 0.525 and the worst $-0.153$; two of 33 rooftops score worse than persistence. The leave-one-system-out models also train on the validation period.

\textbf{Hyperparameters for transfer.} Table~\ref{tab:transfer} and Fig.~\ref{fig:deploy} compare three configurations. The configuration that wins on the chronological split is the worst on unseen rooftops: median $R^2$ 0.700, worst case $-1.03$, four failures. Selecting against held-out systems raises the median to 0.738 and the 10th percentile from 0.322 to 0.525, lifts the worst rooftop to $-0.17$ and halves the failures; it is better on 21 of 33 rooftops (Wilcoxon $p = 0.004$ on seed-averaged $R^2$). Its shallow, regularised trees cannot encode rooftop identity and must learn what rooftops share.

\textbf{Rated capacity and site features.} On unseen rooftops, the capacity ratio $P_{99}/C_\mathrm{p}$ (Section~\ref{sec:data-meta}) correlates with forecast bias at +0.695. A nameplate that overstates effective capacity leads to over-forecasting. The lowest ratio, 0.568, belongs to 94362 ($R^2$ 0.029). The physical chain shows the same dependence (bias correlation +0.850); from nameplate metadata it reaches median $R^2$ 0.712 with six failures, and the zero-shot learner beats it on 25 of 33 rooftops. An ablation separates the site features (Supplementary material). Removing the reported capacity, tilt and azimuth costs 0.075 in $R^2$ on seen rooftops but slightly improves unseen ones (median 0.747 against 0.741). Removing the lagged performance ratio instead leaves seen rooftops unchanged but drops the unseen 10th percentile from 0.525 to 0.330 and triples the failures.

\textbf{Local calibration (E5).} Fig.~\ref{fig:calib} shows median skill against the length of the local window, starting in mid-February. Below about 30 days every correction is harmful: at 3 days, linear rescaling gives skill $-0.157$ against 0.339 uncorrected. From 30 days, linear rescaling is best or near-best, by +0.006 skill at 30 days, +0.028 at 45 and +0.058 at 60. The threshold is seasonal. Scored on a fixed later window, with calibration starting in May, August or November 2025, linear rescaling already helps after 7 days (Supplementary material); short windows are harmful only for the mid-February start. A safe rule is linear rescaling after one week of history, extended to a month when that week falls in a fast seasonal transition.
