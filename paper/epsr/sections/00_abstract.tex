\ifepsr Day-ahead photovoltaic (PV) forecasts for individual rooftops support household energy management, aggregator bidding and distribution-grid operation, but\else Day-ahead photovoltaic (PV) forecasts for individual rooftops support household energy management and aggregator bidding, but\fi\ reported accuracy gains rarely show how close they are to a limit. We diagnose that limit for 33 residential rooftops in Belgium at 15-min resolution over 26 months, with day-ahead numerical weather prediction (NWP) and a test window from mid-February to May. Four analyses are consistent with a limit set by the weather input. Adding the measured output of the three nearest rooftops, a proxy for local weather knowledge, raises $R^2$ from 0.804 to 0.869; the fleet-wide average adds only 0.019. Residual autocorrelation falls from 0.71 at 15 min to 0.11 at 24 h. Irradiance forecast error is equally large at every rooftop but decorrelates with distance, from 0.92 within 10 km to 0.19 beyond 150 km, consistent with errors at the scale of clouds. Removing 70\% of the systematic irradiance bias with model output statistics leaves PV accuracy unchanged on seen and unseen rooftops. Two results should generalise to fleet models built from volunteer data. Scoring the forecast against ERA5, a reanalysis from the same centre, understates its RMSE 1.39-fold relative to the SARAH-3 satellite record. Hyperparameters selected on a chronological split transfer poorly; selecting them against held-out systems halves failures on unseen rooftops. An unseen rooftop receives median $R^2$ 0.741 against 0.804 for rooftops seen in training, and its accuracy depends more on its own recent output and effective capacity than on its reported metadata.
